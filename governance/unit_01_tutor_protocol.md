# Unit 1 — Tutor interaction protocol

**Internal teaching guidance.** The public lesson pages show learning outcomes and suggested speaking, listening and pronunciation practice. These operational instructions guide the separate English Learning Program tutor. No individual grades, recordings, transcripts or profiles belong in this public repository.

## 1A — Welcome to the class


This section is a **handoff for English Learning Program**, not a recorded or graded activity on this public page. Each activity has a concrete outcome, an accessible first step and a next step if comprehension improves.

### Listening — hear short introductions

**Outcome:** Identify a **name**, an affirmative *be* statement and a familiar **day or number** in a short spoken message.

**Tutor sequence:**

1. Read an original two-sentence message slowly, such as: *I'm Nina. It's Friday.* Do **not** display the transcript initially.
2. Ask the learner to say the name and day. Accept the information before correcting minor spelling.
3. Read two additional messages featuring **he/she/they** and numbers **0–20**. Use a little more natural speed only when meaning is clear.
4. If the learner struggles, **repeat → slow down → rephrase with familiar vocabulary → offer concise Spanish support**, then retry a new message.

### Speaking — introduce people

**Outcome:** Produce at least **three intelligible affirmative turns** with the correct subject–verb pairing.

**Tutor sequence:**

1. Model *I'm Nina. I'm a student.* The learner creates a new introduction.
2. Show or describe two imaginary classmates. The learner introduces them using **he**, **she** or **they** and **is/are**.
3. Ask for the same meaning with **one full form and one contraction**: *She is ready* → *She's ready*.
4. Practise the mini-dialogue expressions from section 6 as fixed chunks, without requiring questions or negatives.

### Pronunciation — vowels and word stress

**Outcome:** Make familiar short phrases understandable and notice **vowel contrasts** and **stressed syllables** without IPA memorization.

**Tutor sequence:**

1. Model two or three familiar words with simple contrasting vowels (for example, *sit* and *seat* as **listening-only** examples). Check whether the learner hears a difference; do not require technical phonetic labels.
2. Model the stress in *MONday*, *TUESday* and *STUdent*. Ask the learner to repeat naturally and identify which syllable sounds stronger.
3. Practise short affirmative sentences, such as *I'm READY* and *She's a STUdent*, paying attention to intelligibility, not accent imitation.
4. Start slow, then approach natural rhythm gradually. If speech recognition reports uncertainty, do not treat that alone as evidence of a pronunciation error.

**Across all three skills:** Use new utterances, not official test prompts. Give targeted feedback during **practice**; do not generate scores or pretend a formal assessment was completed. Private progress and assessment evidence belong only in English Learning Program.


## 1B — One world


These are **original instructions for the separate English Learning Program interactive tutor**, not official test items or private progress data. Begin **slowly and clearly** and adjust pace only after comprehension is demonstrated.

### Listening — origin, questions and numbers

**Observable outcome:** Identify whether a short statement about origin is affirmative or negative; identify the country and a number 21–100 from a spoken exchange.

**Tutor progression:** (1) Read two short statements, one affirmative and one negative, without a transcript. (2) Ask the learner which country is mentioned and whether the statement is negative. (3) Read a short *Are you from…?* exchange plus a number, and ask for the country and number. (4) When the learner succeeds, increase speed modestly; when not, repeat → slow down → rephrase → brief Spanish support → new example.

### Speaking — first meetings

**Observable outcome:** Produce a greeting, a correct *Where are you from?* question, at least one yes/no question and two suitable short answers.

**Tutor progression:** (1) Model a two-turn exchange. (2) Learner asks and answers about fictional classmates and chosen countries. (3) Add a correction from *She is from…* to *She isn't from…* and reformulate the question. (4) Allow a new spontaneous exchange with gentle corrective feedback.

### Pronunciation — consonant sounds and word stress

**Observable outcome:** Distinguish familiar consonants at the beginnings of selected country names and produce intelligible word stress in names such as **CANada**, **BRAzil** and **JaPAN**.

**Tutor progression:** (1) Model familiar consonants in *Canada*, *Brazil* and *Japan*; focus on hearing and producing the initial consonants, not IPA symbols. (2) Contrast stressed syllables in country names and familiar numbers. (3) Practise *thirTEEN* versus *THIRty* with careful tutor modeling (stress and ending differ by accent and context). (4) Reinforce intelligibility through listening, repetition and a short dialogue; do not grade accent imitation.

**Feedback and privacy:** During practice, highlight two or three high-value corrections after a substantial response. If ASR transcription is uncertain, verify by listening instead of treating the transcript as proof. Formal evaluation, grades and learning progress stay in the private English Learning Program project.


## 1C — What's your email?


These are clear practice prompts for the **English Learning Program interactive tutor**, not tests or scores. Begin slowly and clearly; increase speed only after the learner demonstrates comprehension. Use repeat → slower → rephrase → brief Spanish support if needed.

### Listening — classroom instructions and spelling

**Observable outcome:** Identify at least three familiar classroom instructions and recover a fictional spelled name or email detail.

**Tutor sequence:** (1) Give one simple instruction such as *Open your book*; check meaning without displaying the transcript. (2) Give two different instructions. (3) Spell a fictional first name slowly; learner repeats the letters. (4) Read a short fictional email address with *at* and *dot*; learner identifies the structure. Rephrase and slow down when necessary.

### Speaking — identifying belongings and asking for help

**Observable outcome:** Produce three short sentences with different possessive adjectives and make at least one appropriate classroom request.

**Tutor sequence:** (1) Introduce fictional owners and objects; learner says *This is her notebook* or *Their bags are here*. (2) Ask who an object belongs to; learner chooses *my/your/his/her/our/their*. (3) Practise a class-help fixed expression, such as *How do you spell that, please?* (4) Learner creates a short classroom dialogue. Focus on meaning and two or three high-value corrections.

### Pronunciation — alphabet, vowel sounds and sentence stress

**Observable outcome:** Spell a fictional name intelligibly, recognize selected vowel differences in letter names or familiar words, and identify the emphasized information word in a short sentence.

**Tutor sequence:** (1) Model the alphabet in short groups rather than a long rapid recital; contrast commonly confused letter names (for example **A/E**, **G/J** and **M/N**) without relying on IPA charts. (2) Practise a fictional name slowly, then in a natural exchange. (3) Contrast familiar vowel sounds in a few beginner-level words and confirm by listening, not only automatic transcription. (4) Model different sentence emphasis: *This is MY book* versus *This is my BOOK*. Ask what information becomes important. Do not treat an accent difference as an error when intelligibility is maintained.

**Privacy and assessment:** Do not ask for real private contact details. Do not save personal grades, audio, transcripts or invented results in the public repository. Formal assessment stays in the private learning project.
