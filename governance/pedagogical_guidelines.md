# English Learning Program — Pedagogical Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0.0 (draft for review)  
**Authority:** Program decisions agreed in chat; textbook indices and samples guide progression and exercise *formats*, not text reproduction.

## 1. Goal and non-negotiables

Build integrated English competence from **Elementary** through **Advanced Plus**, with the ambition of reaching strong C1-level abilities. Treat **Grammar, Vocabulary, Writing, Listening, Speaking, and Pronunciation** as equally important over the full curriculum. Begin at Elementary to locate foundational gaps. Use diagnostic tasks to accelerate known content while preserving missing prerequisites.

**Do not confuse format with learning value:** the public website carries reference material and independent practice; listening/speaking/pronunciation are practiced primarily in interactive voice sessions. These three skills are not excluded from the curriculum or assessments. They may be referenced in web pages as session objectives, without inserting obligatory IPA tables or pretending text alone can teach pronunciation.

## 2. Editorial sources and boundaries

- **Primary pedagogical references:** supplied English File (fourth edition) contents for seven editorial levels; selected Pre-intermediate Grammar Bank and Vocabulary Bank pages; complete Intermediate e-book; sample Advanced Plus pages.
- **Supported findings:** grammar rules with examples, compact tables and immediate exercises; themed visual Vocabulary Banks; contextualized reading and listening/speaking tasks; practical/colloquial scenarios; periodic `Revise and Check`.
- **Origin labels:** record `source_index_confirmed` for a topic explicitly in an index; `source_sample_observed` for a practice pattern observed in supplied pages; `program_extension` for our additions; `pending_source` if an index section is unseen.
- **Copyright:** never republish scans, verbatim textbook exercises, full texts, or illustrations from the books. Write original explanations, sample contexts, questions, answer keys and images. Reference book titles and lesson identifiers only as bibliographic traceability.
- **Accuracy:** when indexed but not fully documented in uploaded source, say so; do not claim to have observed absent instruction. Check edge cases, regional usage and alternate valid answers.

## 3. Language of instruction

- Default teaching prose: **English**, appropriate to the learner's actual competence, not arbitrarily C1 at Elementary.
- Every **central concept** has a concise explanation in **English and Spanish**; retain this bilingual anchor at advanced levels.
- Supporting explanations may use Spanish to resolve persistent ambiguity, then return to English and request reformulation.
- Contrast grammar, register, meaning and usage; explain *why*, not only *which answer*.
- Avoid long Spanish paraphrases that replace active English practice.

## 4. Public lesson structure

1. **Identity and prerequisites:** level, book-unit/lesson crosswalk, learning objectives, concepts previously required.
2. **Bilingual concept:** concise English definition + Spanish equivalent.
3. **Grammar:** statements/questions/negatives, subject patterns, time markers, meaning, form, exceptions, common traps. Tables where genuinely useful.
4. **Contextualized examples:** everyday situations; occasionally relevant technology/work; examples tagged standard, informal, workplace when distinction is material.
5. **Vocabulary:** core topic terms with meanings, collocations, useful chunks, examples; purposeful images when they improve understanding.
6. **Everyday English:** natural idioms, contractions, phrasal verbs and modals at an appropriate level, with clear register/frequency cautions. Do **not** equate slang with native-like proficiency.
7. **Guided self-study practice:** up to **20 grammar exercises per topic** (not necessarily per entire book unit); progressive task types, original answer key and rationale. Writing tasks separate from the grammar-exercise cap.
8. **Writing application:** one short productive task and a longer optional extension, with an evaluation checklist.
9. **Quick reference:** concise printable structure/usage table, related lessons, frequent pitfalls, and cross-links to verb / phrasal-verb references.
10. **Interactive handoff:** specific oral/listening/pronunciation objectives and relevant contexts; no graded speaking test on the public page.

The website may divide this structure across tabs/pages. Keep titles, navigation, IDs and citation-to-source metadata consistent.

## 5. Practice progression and answer handling

Exercise count is **adaptive**: 8–12 can suffice for simple items; 20 is the upper bound per grammar topic. Suggested mix for a full 20: recognition (4), construction (5), error correction (4), contrasts (4), free production (3). This distribution is **our design suggestion**, not an English File prescription. Increase ambiguity control and provide accepted alternatives in keys. A public answer key is for ungraded practice only.

**Official assessments** are generated separately by the teaching project, do not reveal answers before submission, and use new items or unseen contexts to assess transfer—not memorization of web answers.

### Modal / phrasal-verb spiral

Introduce simple high-frequency forms early as communicative chunks, then revisit their meanings, particles, separability, transitivity and register as proficiency grows. Example trajectory (proposed, not a textbook claim):

- Elementary: `can/can't`, `get up`, `wake up`, `turn on/off` in authentic short clauses.
- Pre-intermediate: `have to`, `should`, `look for`, `find out` and routine politeness.
- Intermediate: `might`, `must`, `could`, `put off`, `figure out`, with context and alternatives.
- Upper-intermediate/Advanced: modal deduction and past modals, nuanced phrasal verbs, appropriateness, idiom and collocation contrasts.

Avoid overwhelming beginners with morphological theory or rare phrasal verbs.

### Work / technology integration

Occasionally include examples of data pipelines, SQL queries, dashboards, code reviews, incidents and team communication. The *language* is being assessed, not specialist engineering knowledge. Always retain sufficiently many nontechnical scenarios for broad everyday fluency. Technical terms must be comprehensible from context or explicitly taught.

## 6. Purposeful visual policy

Every asset has a teaching purpose: identify objects/actions, contrast demonstratives, show spatial prepositions, explain timelines, depict a sequence, or contextualize vocabulary. Require an `alt` description and teaching note for each asset. Prefer original assets over copied textbook pictures; diagrams and precise typography over generic decorative stock. Do not add images just to fill space. Test mobile readability, contrast, labels and ambiguity. For demonstratives, depict *distance + number* explicitly; for time tenses, distinguish event, interval and reference point.

## 7. Live teacher modes

- **Learn:** explain with checks for understanding and short bilingual anchors. Interleave recall.
- **Practice:** one item at a time, immediate targeted correction, record misconception category, increase or reduce difficulty.
- **Conversation:** speech/listening exchange on current and prior units; adapt speech speed and vocabulary; correct 2–4 high-value issues after a meaningful response rather than continuously interrupting. Provide a safe repeat → slower → rephrase → Spanish-support ladder for comprehension failures.
- **Assessment:** clear rubric and task scope, no hints during scoring, record criterion-level evidence and uncertainty, preserve attempts.

In every mode, speaking and listening begin in Elementary. Increase natural speed and accent variety gradually based on observed comprehension, not rigidly by chapter. A non-native accent is acceptable; pronunciation targets **intelligibility** and intelligible rhythm/stress rather than accent erasure. ASR transcripts are not reliable proof of all phonetic distinctions.

## 8. Cumulative review and acceleration

Use active recall, interleaving and spaced repetition. Suggested initial checkpoints after learning: **day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30**, adjusted for actual recall and schedule. Combine previous grammar, vocabulary, modals/phrasal verbs and meaningful speaking/listening tasks. On persistent errors: diagnose → targeted mini-lesson → new contexts → retest → next review.

A strong unit diagnostic can shorten guided instruction, **not bypass the six-skill evidence** or critical prerequisites. Full level mastery needs retained abilities; see `assessment_framework.md`.

## 9. Quality checklist / definition of done

- [ ] Topic and location verified against a supplied index or explicitly tagged as program extension.
- [ ] Explanations are original and accurate; any tricky rule has carefully reviewed examples.
- [ ] Main concept bilingual; other explanations primarily accessible English.
- [ ] Each example is natural, register-appropriate and uses the intended rule.
- [ ] Exercises have unambiguous instructions and documented accepted alternatives.
- [ ] Vocabulary includes useful collocations/chunks, not only translations.
- [ ] Purposeful visuals pass legibility, alt-text and meaning checks.
- [ ] Speaking/listening/pronunciation handoff exists and is level appropriate.
- [ ] Prerequisite IDs and cumulative-review tags are declared.
- [ ] Website page renders on mobile; local preview succeeds; links and images load.
- [ ] No copyrighted source scans or original textbook tasks are published.
